## 1. Conversion best practices (B2B services)

- **One page, one goal.** Removing nav links and secondary offers concentrates attention on one action. `[principle]` → Minimal nav (anchor links only), one CTA.
- **Outcome headlines beat category headlines.** "Cut support costs 30% in 90 days" outperforms "AI Consulting Services". `[principle]` → Hero = outcome + timeframe.
- **Above-the-fold must answer: what, for whom, why trust, what next.** `[principle]` → Headline, subhead, CTA, trust line all visible without scroll.
- **Repeat the CTA after every persuasion block** (after offer, after process, after comparison, at end). `[principle]` → ≥ 4 identical CTAs.
- **First-person CTA wording** ("Book my call") tends to beat second-person ("Book your call"). `[unverified]` widely cited test (Unbounce/ContentVerve) → Use "Book my free AI call".
- **Specific numbers increase credibility** — odd/precise numbers (37%) read as measured, round ones as estimated. `[principle]`
- **Microcopy under the button** lowering perceived cost ("30 min, no obligation") reduces anxiety at the click. `[principle]`
- **PAS / problem-first structure** works for aware-but-frustrated buyers. `[principle]` → Sections 3–4 before solution.
- **Mobile sticky CTA** keeps the action reachable during long scrolls. `[principle]`

## 2. High-ticket consulting offer design

- Buyers don't buy "hours"; they buy a **packaged outcome with a defined scope and timeframe**. `[principle]` → 3–4 named packages.
- **Productised entry offer** (paid audit/sprint) lowers the commitment of the first purchase vs. a big retainer. `[principle]` → "AI Opportunity Sprint" as the entry package.
- **Free call must deliver standalone value** (a written plan) or it reads as a sales call. `[principle]` → Deliver a 1-page AI Opportunity Map after the call.
- **Price anchoring / "starting at"** filters unqualified leads and builds trust; hiding all price signals increases tyre-kicker leads. `[principle]` → Give ranges in FAQ.
- **Risk reversal** (guarantee tied to a milestone) is a strong lever for services where outcome uncertainty is the #1 fear. `[principle]`

## 3. AI-consulting buyer psychology

- Most companies have experimented with genAI but few see P&L impact. MIT NANDA, *The GenAI Divide: State of AI in Business 2025* reports ~95% of enterprise genAI pilots show no measurable P&L impact. (Verify before quoting.)
- **Core fears:** wasting budget on a pilot that goes nowhere; data leakage / compliance; staff resistance; vendor lock-in; hype merchants who can't build.
- **Core desires:** measurable ROI, hours saved, faster response times, a clear roadmap, a team that can maintain it after the consultants leave.
- **Trigger events:** board/CEO asks "what's our AI plan?"; a competitor ships an AI feature; a hiring freeze with growing workload; a failed internal pilot.
- Buyers are skeptical of "AI experts" — **show the build, not just strategy**. `[principle]`

## 4. Competitor patterns

- Typical AI agency pages: gradient-brain hero, "Unlock the power of AI", a grid of buzzword services (chatbots, ML, NLP, computer vision), no numbers, generic "Contact us".
- Big consultancies: strategy decks, long timelines, high price, implementation subcontracted.
- Freelancers / no-code builders: fast and cheap, but no strategy, security, or change management.
- **Gap to own:** strategy + build + adoption by one small senior team, with a fixed-scope first win in ~30 days.

## 5. Form & lead-capture design

- Fewer fields → higher completion; for high-ticket services, **1–2 qualifying fields** improve lead quality without much drop. `[principle]` → Name, work email, company, "what do you want AI to fix?" (select), optional note.
- Labels above fields, not placeholder-only. `[principle]` (accessibility + error recovery)
- Inline validation on blur, clear error text. `[principle]`
- **Say what happens next** right after submit ("We'll reply within 1 business day with 3 time slots"). `[principle]`
- Offer an **alternative path** (external form/calendar link) for people who prefer it. `[principle]`
- Honeypot field beats CAPTCHA for friction. `[principle]`

## 6. Trust & proof for a small/new firm

- Use **specific mini case studies** (problem → what we built → number) even if anonymised ("a 120-person logistics company"). `[principle]`
- **Founder/team credibility** (past roles, shipped systems) substitutes for big logos. `[principle]`
- Security signals: NDA by default, data stays in your cloud, SOC2-aligned practices — only claim what's true.
- Testimonials need **name, role, company, and a specific result**; vague praise adds little. `[principle]`
